# Juice Oracle - 11/4/26

:

## Introduction

:

Juice is an oracle I have been iterating on for a long time. It is intended to be travel friendly and minimal,
yet versatile and complete. It is a dense collection of generic tables from various sources that can be used
to enhance your roleplaying experience. There is a large focus on immersion and NPC interaction, because
I find that this is where I get the most enjoyment out of a solo session.
If you are familiar with the Mythic system, this oracle will be easy to use.

## Differences between Pop and Smoothie

:

The previous Juice Oracle version was jam-packed, but this made it a bit hard to read and a bit hard to use. After my work with the Nimble Solo community, I decided to revise, simplify, and streamline Juice a bit. I couldn't get all the ideas I wanted into one cohesive page, so I decided "why not two?"

:

And thus we have Juice Pop and Juice Smoothie.

:

Juice Pop is closer in spirit to what Juice has always been; a set of generic modular tables that can be applied to many different situations. A toolbox of sorts. It contains the essence of what I use for solo play, and follows the philosophy of "Less is More".

:

I would say that Juice Pop is a bit more structured but requires a bit more interpretation, while Juice Smoothie is intended to be more freeflow, spitting out fully flavored prompts. They are both meant to be standalone as well as complement each other.

## Prep

### Requirements

:

Speaking of Dice, this version of Juice only needs:

- 2d6
- 2d10
- 2d20


> No goofy Fate Dice this time around.

### Printing

:

I did my best to adjust the margins to fit most printers I can test on. Simply print in color, double sided, flipping on the long edge.

### Folding

#### Juice Pop

:

- Position the paper so it is landscape, with "Quest" in the lower left corner.
- Fold the top edge down. You will see "Immersion" on the far left, and "Consequence" on the far right.
- Fold the left side over the right side. You will see a sideways "Yes/No Oracle" and the "Name" table.
- Unfold that last one, then fold the left to the center fold line. You will see "Name" on the left, and "Consequence" on the right.
- Fold the right side to the middle. You will see "Name" and "Inspiration"
- Fold in half like a book. "Yes/No" will be sideways on the front, and "Random Event" on the back.

> (Optional, Recommended) Open it back up, cut a line between "What do they say?" and "Creating a Challenge" to the middle fold. This is so you can open just the Quest side or just the Exploration side as needed.

:

Some of the tables are sideways to make it easier to read (larger font). If you are holding the pocketfold in your left hand looking at the Y/N oracle, you can flip it over to the Random Event table pretty naturally.  

::

#### Juice Smoothie

:

- Position the paper such that "What is your Quest" is facing you and at the top. 
- Fold it down, you will see "What is the current Situation?"
- Fold the left side over to the right in half. You will see "Yes/No Oracle" and "Name"
- Unfold that last one, then fold the left side to the center line. You will see "Name" on the left, and half of the situation table on the right.
- Fold the right side over to the middle line, you will see "Name" and "Color"
- Fold in half like a book, so "Yes/No Oracle" is on the front, and "Inspiration" is on the back.

## Play

### Mythic Primer 

As Juice is based on the Mythic system, it helps to understand how Mythic works. I’m not going to go into
a lot of detail here, you should read Mythic GME 2e. But hopefully, the following is enough for you to get
started.

#### Fate Questions

The first cornerstone of Mythic is Fate Questions. These are Yes/No questions that you ask about the
world. Is the tavern busy? Mythic uses its Fate Chart to determine the answer as Yes or No. You can boil
anything down to a series of Yes/No questions to help determine what is happening.

#### Discover Meaning

But what about questions where the answer isn’t a simple Yes/No? What does the monster do? For this,
Mythic provides a large collection of Meaning Tables which are each lists of 100 words. You roll on a
meaning table and use the word pairings to spark your imagination for what the answer could be.
Meaning tables are designed to inspire, and you need to get creative to discover what your roll means.

#### Random Events

Asking a Fate Question opens the possibility for triggering a Random Event. When this happens, you roll
on a special table that dictates how your story might change. Random Events automatically prompt you to
do bookkeeping, integrate items from your lists into the adventure, or prompt for immersion checks and
NPC actions.

#### Lists

Mythic uses Lists to keep track of the “state” of your adventure. There is a list for Threads and a list for
Characters. Mythic GME 2e uses some really cool weighted list templates, and if you have access to them I
would recommend going this route. Otherwise, you can simply keep track of your lists using index cards
numbered 1-10.

##### Threads

A Thread is like a side-story or side-quest. Well, your main quest is also a thread. Basically, your adventure
is composed of different threads that are interwoven to create your story. As you play, various threads will
16
“start” as you discover things in the world around you. You can choose to follow threads to see where they
go, or simply let them die off if they are not interesting. Random Events will prompt you to interact with or
clean up threads as you play.
Threads are similar to Vows in Ironsworn.

##### Characters

As you meet NPCs, you might choose to keep significant ones on your Character list. Random Events will
prompt you to interact with characters or cause them to take actions that affect the world around you.
Mythic also calls significant objects “Characters”.

##### Events

Sometimes there are certain things you want to have happen in your game. Mythic uses a powerful tool
called a Keyed Scene to handle this. Juice simplifies the concept by using an Event list coupled with a
Random Event trigger of Keyed Event, which will invoke an event from your list. You can use this to
customize the types of things that will happen during your adventure.

##### Locations

Mythic doesn’t use an official Locations list, but basically this is a list of locations in your world. This list will
be referenced by Juice’s Remote Event trigger, and the Settlement News feed. Similar
to Characters, this helps keep Locations that you have generated involved in your story.

#### Scenes

Mythic breaks your adventure into “Scenes”, much like watching a movie. Each scene will have some sort of
action or event occurring, interacting with certain people or objects. This all becomes the context for your
Fate Questions or Discover Meaning checks.
As your adventure progresses, you will naturally have an expectation for what the next scene might look
like. Mythic inserts some randomness between scenes to alter or interrupt your expectation to keep things
interesting.

### Juice Pop Tables

:

#### Yes/No & Next Scene

:

The main Yes/No mechanic. Simple, you have likely already used this before. Juice Pop has you roll 2d6 every time, so doubles can trigger random events. Normally you look at the leftmost die for the result, but can use highest/lowest number based on if the odds are likely/unlikely.

```
? Is the tavern busy?
d: 2d6 -> 1 6 -> No, And...
=> Nope, you are the only one here. The barkeep was considering closing for the night.
```

:

Also here is the main game loop for scenes, and a table that can change the expected scene to keep things interesting.

::

#### Random Event

:

Rolling doubles on any Y/N question triggers a random event.

:

This is a d20 table, and numbers 11-20 all trigger your companion to take action. This is intended to make a party feel more alive; you control your "main" character and random events might trigger your other characters to do things.

:

If you don't have another character in the party, just roll 1d10.

::

#### Name

:

Every toolkit needs a name generator. Pop comes with one that is a bit more sophisticated than Smoothie.

:

You get a template that tells you _how_ to roll on columns 1/2/3. The `+/-` on column 3 means roll 1d10 on the high/low values, while the `o/a/i` means append that letter.

:

The `(x)` on rows 1-5 of column 1 are used to add constanants when using column 1 multiple times.

```
# Template: 1d20 -> 19 -> 123-i
d: 2d20 + 1d10 -> 1 6 1 -> a net an
=> Anetani
```

```
# Template: 1d20 -> 11 -> 111
d: 3d20 -> 2 9 5 -> e ma su
=> Emasu
```

::

#### Inspiration

:

A generic pair of words for giving a bit more detail. Roll this whenever you are stuck to help trigger some ideas. 

```
# Inspiration
d: 2d20 -> 8 17 -> Helpful Shadow
```

:

There is also a Symbol table, a set of nouns that are open for interpretation in any scenario. Use it for establishments, monsters, omens, etc.

```
? What is the name of the Tavern?
# Color + Symbol
d: 2d20 -> 10 3 -> Clear Frost, Blade
=> The Frosted Dagger
```

#### Immersion

:

Immerse yourself in your character; see what they see, feel what they feel. You can roll on this at almost any time to add flavor to the scene.

```
# Immersion
d: 5d10 -> 4 2 2 1 9
=> You hear fire behind you and it causes anger because you were warned about it.
```

::

#### Dialog

:

Dialog with NPCs is a great way to get more information about the world to your character. But what do they say?

:

"Hey, what do you think of the war?"

```
# Dialog
4d10 -> 4 2 3 5
=> Their distant refusal concerns the past and suggests neutrality.
```

:

"I'm tired of everyone talking about the war, leave me out of it."

::

#### Challenge

:

Adventures are more fun where there is a challenge to overcome. You can use this to create a challenge during a scene.

:

You can use 1d20 to pick a skill, but I personally like to roll up _two_ challenges; one physical and one mental. This gives me two success paths, and if I fail both challenges then I roll the consequence.

```
# Challenge
d: 4d10 (Physical) -> 6 4 8 6
d: 4d10 (Mental) -> 7 0 6 7
=> Use Perception to overcome a foe in order to seize a retreat.
=> Use Religion to overcome a vital link in order to reveal the ritual.
```

::

#### Consequence

:

You don't always win, and failure leads to consequences. But consequences are just opportunities to make the story more interesting.

```
# Consequence
d: 1d20 -> 19
=> Your reputation shifts
```

:

So for the previous challenge, lets say you were a cleric, attempting to ask your deity with help identifying the source of the necromantic ritual. Failing that challenge perhaps means you were unable to win over the favor of your god, and thus lose reputation with them, affecting your ability to ask for more favors in the future.

#### Quest

:

With the main building blocks covered, its time to create a quest and set off on the adventure.

:

I like this table, because it can be used 3 ways:

- 3d10 for a simple side quest
- 4d10 for an added complication
- 10d10 for a full quest giver and destination

The Person and Location tables are split off and useful on their own as well.

```
# Simple side quest
d: 3d10 -> 9 1 0
=> Rescue someone from the abandoned villain.
```

```
# Quest with complication
d: 4d10 -> 7 5 1 8
=> Negotiate with the decayed anomaly, though nothing is as it seems.
```

```
# Full Quest
d: 10d10 -> 0 7 2 3 1 9 3 6 4 3
=> A wounded priest, concerned about experience, asks you to escape from the abandoned plant in the Crypt of Forgotten Fury, before they escape.
```

:

Normally I like to start my quests in a settlement, however things like this make me feel like the start of the quest is that the Priest and I are already deep in the dungeon and need to get out. Nothing wrong with the quest changing your assumptions.

::

#### Settlement

:

I treat settlements as safe havens. Give them a name, some places to interact with, and treat them as a source of information. The quest hub if you will.

```
# Settlement
d: Name 2d10 -> 3 0 -> Lostwood
d: Establishments 1d6 -> 2
- 1d10 -> 8 -> Temple, Color + Symbol: 2d20 -> 14 19 -> Temple of the Midnight Snake
- 1d10 -> 7 -> Courier, Color + Symbol: 2d20 -> 6 8 -> The Electric Dragon Messenging Service
d: News 1d10 -> 5 -> Succession
```

:

So perhaps here, upon arrival we learn from the newspaper boy outside The Electric Dragon that the leader of the Temple of the Midnight Snake has just stepped down, causing rumors to spread around town as people wonder why.

::

#### Color

:

The color table is an inspiration table in and of itself. I chose to give each color a descriptive adjective to evoke the imagination, intended to be interpreted in many different ways.

:

Use color to give flavor to weapons, monsters, potions. Use it to name establishments, define the flags of the kingdoms or knights, or determine the type of magical spell being cast.

#### Wilderness Exploration

:

I recommend starting your adventure in a Settlement in the Grassland. Roll a description. Each time you transition to a new area, roll on the Ladder and move up or down to the new environment.

:

Lets say we start in the "Haunted Grassland". When we enter the next area, we roll an 8 on the ladder table, and a 9 on the description table. This means we are moving from the "Haunted Grassland" to the "Dying Forest".

:

For encounter, you roll with Disadvantage each time, until you get doubles, then roll with Advantage from that point on.

:

This mechanism does two things: first, you get somewhat realistic biome transitions, and second you get somewhat realistic danger levels. When travelling from the settlement, early encounters will typically be less dangerous things as you are still close to civilization. As you travel, things will get more difficult, but you will get closer to your destination.

:

Example:

- Start: Haunted Grassland
- Ladder: 8, Description: 9 -> "Dying Forest". Encounter (1d10 @ Disadvantage): 4,7 -> Road/River
- Ladder: 1, Description: 6 -> "Shadowed Hills". Encounter (1d10 @ Disadvantage): 2,5 -> Weather
- Ladder: 6, Description: 8 -> "Verdant Hills". Encounter (1d10 @ Disadvantage): 3,3 -> Immersion (Doubles! Switch to Advantage)
- Ladder: 4, Description: 7 -> "Pristine Cavern". Encounter (1d10 @ Advantage): 1,5 -> Monster
- Ladder: 2, Description: 3 -> "Luminous Mountains". Encounter (1d10 @ Advantage): 8,9 -> Destination/Dungeon

> Where is the weather table? There isn't one this time around. I personally found that weather essentially boiled down to either its nice out, or there is rain/snow/storm. So I personally just treat "Weather" as something specific to whatever environment I am in now; Snow if in the arctic, Blistering Sun in the desert, rain in the swamps, etc.

::

#### Dungeon Exploration

:

Dungeon Exploration does the same "Disadvantage -> Doubles -> Advantage" pattern, but applied to both the Next Area and Encounter.

:

For this reason, I named them "Early" and "Late". In "Early", you roll Disadvantage on both columns. Doubles on the Next Area will transition from Early to Late, and from that point on you roll Advantage on both columns. Doubles on Encounter should be treated as a Random Event.

:

This has the effect of creating a sprawling dungeon with lots of exploration opportunities early on, but as you get deeper things get more dangerous, until you find the objective. At that point, the "Next Area" tends to be creating connections and exits so you can get back out easily.

:

Example:

- 

> There is only one objective, if you don't roll it then the last reachable area is the objective. If you roll a second objective, treat it as a Key. Keys are like Zelda-style keys in that they are one use only, but can open any locked door.

::

#### Treasure

:

Roll 4d6 to get a descriptive random object or treasure. Good for any loot you might find, or things you might see in a shop for sale.

### Juice Smoothie Tables

:

#### Yes/No Oracle

:

Juice Smoothie is designed to get out of your way and let you go with the flow. So there aren't any random events built in to interrupt you. This page has a simple d6 based Y/N answer, and some of my favorite tips for playing solo.

#### Inspiration

:

Instead of word pairs, Smoothie gives you micro-prompts that add a little flavor to whatever is going on.

#### Name

:

Its the same name table as Pop, but simplified without the template. 3d20 gets you a quick name. 

#### Color

:

Same color table as Pop; this is a really good table that can be applied to a ton of things, so it gets put front and center. It is essentially a second inspiration table.

#### Situation

:

The core of Smoothie. Roll across each column to generate a detailed sentence describing the current situation. This incorporates Immersion, Encounters, Features, and Challenges all in one.

#### Quest

:

This gives you your starting spot and uses the same quest pattern as Pop.

#### Exploration

:

For both Wilderness and Dungeon, you just roll 2d20 and what you get is what you get. The Wilderness Environment column _is_ ordered like a ladder if you choose, but the intent is pure random.

#### Dialog

:

Again, 2d20 to see what that NPC is talking about. Could be your companion or anyone else.

#### Examples

:

Smoothie is designed to generate full sentences:

>You start your adventure in a Fortified Small Town near a great tree. A journey occurred today, causing Hope.

>A Lost Mage, concerned about Treasure, asks you to Escape from the Toxic Remains in the Crypt of Shrouded Fury, under false pretenses.

>They are talking about negative news regarding an allied npc.

>You enter a luminous rocky mountain range. Fog hovers just out of sight, disclosing a well. The silence is suddenly broken.

>You find a narrow passage with a locked side door. Webs cling to every surface beyond reach, hiding ruins. Someone is exposed to danger.

:

This is based off the "Fluid Scene Structure" in Mythic Magazine 34.

### Flow

:

The basic flow of play for both Juice Pop and Juice Smoothie is:

- Start in a settlement
- Generate a quest; the objective will be in a dungeon
- Explore the wilderness to find the dungeon
- Explore the dungeon to find the objective

:

Of course, this can expand into sandbox play. Wilderness exploration will lead you to more settlements, which may lead to more quests, and it spirals out from there.

:

As for individual scenes, the basic flow in Juice Pop is this:

- Determine the next Expected Scene, whatever you think will logically happen next.
- Roll 1d6 on **Next Scene** table to see if something changes your expectations
  - Alter: Add or Remove a **Symbol**, interpret it to be a character, object, or event that changes your expected scene.
  - Interrupt: Roll **RE** + **Inspiration** to completely change the scene you were anticipating.
- Set up a **Challenge**; Each scene should have something for your character to do.
- At the end of the scene, update your thread/character lists (I use notecards)

:

For Juice Smoothie, its a little more freeflow.  
As you explore, you roll a **Situation** and deal with it however you wish. "Scenes" are more fluid.
